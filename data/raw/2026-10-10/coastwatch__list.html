SEQ Fishing Reports

This report is compiled every Thursday afternoon. If you see a report that interests you, call the info source for more detailed info and bait, tackle and hire boat availability.

Fishing Report for 9 October 2026

Hervey Bay / Fraser Island

Mackerel continue to dominate catches around Hervey Bay, with plenty of broadbar and school mackerel being caught throughout the region.

Whiting are fishing well along the local beaches and across the flats of the Great Sandy Straits. There is also plenty of bait along the Hervey Bay beaches at present, attracting schools of mackerel close to shore.

In the Great Sandy Straits, both winter and summer whiting are on the chew, with flathead holding around the creek mouths. Grunter are being caught in the creeks and throughout the Mary River system, while threadfin salmon, blue salmon and barramundi are all active through the straits and local river systems.

Mangrove jacks are firing in the western creeks and throughout the Burrum system, where quality grunter and whiting are also being caught in the middle reaches.

Offshore, coral trout are biting well on the shallow reefs, while anglers avoiding the sharks are still finding grassy sweetlip and other reef species. Spanish mackerel continue to be caught around Sandy Cape Shoals.

Billfish activity is gradually building, with black marlin, striped marlin and yellowfin tuna all being reported from the shelf. The heavy tackle season is showing signs of improvement, although it is still early days.

Fraser Island reports remain patchy, while sand crabs are being caught around Waddy Creek and through parts of the straits. Boaties should also be aware of increasing amounts of “snot weed” north of the banks and throughout Platypus Bay, where it is affecting both fishing and boating activities.

Tin Can Bay

The new moon is setting up some excellent tides this weekend, and with northerly winds forecast, water temperatures should continue to climb.

The big evening high tides will be ideal for chasing quality whiting up on the flats tight against the mangroves. Fresh yabbies remain hard to beat, so it’s worth getting out early to pump a few on the afternoon low. Beach worms are also proving highly effective and continue to account for plenty of fish.

The large afternoon low tides will create plenty of opportunities for flathead anglers, with many of the larger drains becoming accessible as water levels drop. There’s also the chance of encountering threadfin salmon and even the odd barramundi in these areas.

Mangrove jacks should be firing on the combination of warm water, larger tides and northerly winds. Soft plastics in the 3-5 inch range worked deep into the snags on the back end of the run-out tide are producing fish. Bream and cod are also active around the same structure.

Grunter continue to fish well throughout the creek systems. Prawn-style lures and soft vibes are working nicely, while anglers fishing baits are finding success with strips of squid and mullet.

Further afield, reports of school and spotted mackerel have come from around Big Mick and Inskip Point. Traditional trolling methods such as planers rigged with small spoons are working, while small hardbodies in the 80-120mm range are also worth a try.

Noosa

Fishing has been fairly consistent through the Noosa River this week, with bream the standout species throughout the Noosaville reaches and up towards the First Lake.

A couple of quality flathead have also been reported around Tewantin and Goat Island, while plenty of smaller flathead in the 35-40cm range are being caught between the river mouth and Woods Bay on the drift.

For bait anglers, prawns and worms continue to be the most reliable options and are accounting for most of the catches.

North Reef continues to produce quality snapper, pearl perch, kingfish and Moses perch. With plenty of current pushing across the wider grounds, the closer reefs are proving the more fishable option at present and are holding plenty of fish.

Whale numbers remain high offshore, so boaties should continue to keep a careful lookout when travelling to and from the grounds.

Flathead continue to feature strongly in the lower estuary, with good numbers of fish being caught throughout the system.

A variety of trevally species are also active through Noosa Sound, providing plenty of action for lure and bait anglers alike.

There are still a few queenfish around, although getting them to the boat has been a challenge, with sharks proving a persistent nuisance once fish are hooked.

Maroochydore

A quieter week on the Maroochy, but there are still a few fish being caught. Whiting are coming from around Chambers Island, while bream and queenfish are being reported from Cod Hole.

A few flathead are also being caught around Eudlo Creek.

Mooloolaba

Offshore, Murphy’s Reef is producing pearl perch and cobia, while a couple of quality wahoo have been reported from around the Fairway Beacon.

In the Maroochy River, some excellent flathead are being caught around the Bli Bli Bridge, while quality sand whiting are coming from the river mouth on live beach worms.

The Mooloolah River is producing bream, trevally and jewfish around the trawler area, while mangrove jacks are becoming more active around canal pontoons and other structure.

A few mud crabs have also been reported from around Bli Bli.

Caloundra / Pumicestone Passage

Both summer and winter whiting are being caught in good numbers around the Rous Channel and Chain Banks.

Some quality flathead are coming from the mouths of Lota Creek and Tingalpa Creek, while school mackerel are showing up around the Ormiston and Willow grounds.

Mud crabs are being reported from Lota Creek and Tingalpa Creek, while sand crabs are being caught just offshore from the Wynnum-Manly foreshore.

Despite a fresh northerly making conditions challenging, anglers fishing the 80-metre grounds have been rewarded with a quality mixed bag.

Bottom fishing with mullet fillets and pilchards has produced red emperor, goldband snapper, pearl perch, snapper, tuskfish and hussar, with those prepared to work through the conditions finding a solid feed of reef fish.

Northern Moreton Bay

The Caboolture and Pine river systems are fishing well for flathead and bream, particularly around the river mouths. A few jewfish are also being caught from the deeper holes, while crabbers are finding some success in the deeper channels and creek mouths.

Around Redcliffe, quality snapper and grunter continue to be caught, along with a few school mackerel. There have also been reports of some decent jewfish from the area.

Whiting are being caught around Nudgee Beach and Cabbage Tree Creek, while threadfin salmon are showing up further south around the Jacobs Well region.

With weather windows opening up, offshore anglers should be looking towards Hutchies for Spanish mackerel and spotted mackerel. Further north, mangrove jacks are beginning to show up throughout the creeks of Pumicestone Passage, while whiting are being caught around the mouth of Ningi Creek.

Moreton Bay Offshore

Plenty of variety on the reefs with dolphin fish turning up in good numbers. These caught around Point Lookout.

Redcliffe Peninsula

Bream, yellowfin whiting, flathead and the odd jewfish are coming from the Pine River, local jetties and around the Newport canal entrance.

Yellowfin whiting and flathead are also being caught along the Scarborough beaches and Redcliffe Spit.

Jewfish and snapper are being caught from the jetties, rocky points, beaches and the inshore reefs, while grunter are showing up around the inshore reefs and canal mouths.

Mangrove jacks continue to be reported from the canals and throughout the Pine River system.

Some trevally are also working bait schools in Bramble Bay.

Wynnum / Manly

Both summer and winter whiting are being caught in good numbers around the Rous Channel and Chain Banks.

Some quality flathead are coming from the mouths of Lota Creek and Tingalpa Creek, while school mackerel are showing up around the Ormiston and Willow grounds.

Mud crabs are being reported from Lota Creek and Tingalpa Creek, while sand crabs are being caught just offshore from the Wynnum-Manly foreshore.

Moreton Bay / Jumpinpin

Moreton Bay / Southern Moreton Bay

Recent trips in Moreton Bay have produced some quality fish, even if the overall numbers have been a little patchy at times. Around Peel Island, anglers are finding solid squire in the 35-45cm range along the shallow reef edges, particularly on the western and north-western sides of the island. Lightly weighted soft plastics have been the standout presentation. Small schools of mac tuna are also moving through the bay, but despite plenty of surface activity they have been frustratingly difficult to convince into striking.

Through the southern bay islands, flathead are holding around the drains and run-offs near Garden Island on the lower tides, with fish generally ranging from 40-65cm. Threadfin salmon numbers have improved around Russell Island, Long Island and nearby drop-offs, with some quality fish moving onto the bite during the higher stages of the tide.

Jumpinpin

Jumpinpin continues to produce outstanding flathead fishing, with recent trips accounting for well over 100 fish across several days. The key has been patience, with fish feeding in short but intense bite windows followed by long quieter periods. Areas around Tipplers, Bedrooms, Gold Bank and nearby weed-lined flats have all been producing.

Most fish are holding right along the weed edges, and often actually within the weed itself. Lightly weighted 1/8oz to 1/6oz jigheads have been particularly effective for working soft plastics through this structure. While there haven’t been huge numbers of trophy fish, there are plenty of flathead in the 50-70cm range, regular fish into the 80cm bracket and enough bigger fish to keep anglers excited.

Mulloway numbers remain lower than expected for this time of year, although there are still fish being caught around the Pin Bar, Crusoe Hole and the Wedge. Most fish are in the 70-90cm range, with anglers still waiting for the larger metre-plus models to arrive in significant numbers.

The quality fish of the week have been giant trevally, with several exceptional fish encountered recently. One fish stretched into the low 80cm range and would have pushed close to 10kg, while another quality GT was landed in the 70cm class. Tailor were active earlier in the week around the tide changes before the dirty water arrived, and mangrove jacks are beginning to show throughout the canals, Coomera River and around the rock walls and pontoons of Sovereign Island.

Jumpinpin

No report this week.

Whiting have been the standout species this week, with plenty of fish pushing the 40cm mark being caught, particularly on live bloodworms.

Flathead are still featuring in catches, although the bite has slowed a little following the Flathead Classic. Most fish have been around the 50cm mark, with anglers still finding enough fish to keep things interesting.

A few mulloway are being caught along the southern beaches of North Stradbroke Island, while the occasional threadfin salmon is also turning up in the same area.

South Stradbroke Island

Gold Coast Broadwater

Some great fishing last week with most people catching a feed. It’s a great time of year for fishing in the Broadwater. Now that school is back, the public holiday passed us and the Flathead Classic done, hopefully it will calm down around here. In saying that, how good are the young fishers in their tiny tinnies in the Seaway!

Morning high tides and the new moon this weekend should see whiting moving up onto the banks. Last week they were found from the Council Chambers down to Wave Break Island in the Nerang River. To the north, they were in the Coomera River from Paradise Point to the mouth and near the mouth of the Pimpama River. There have also been great catches around the Pin, particularly on the Green and Gold Banks.

It’s not a bad idea to put a couple of pots out, with a few sand crabs being caught in the Nerang River, around the Aldershots and in front of Runaway Bay. Mud crabs are also being caught in the upper reaches of the Coomera and Pimpama rivers.

Flathead have been caught in most locations, but finding clean water has been the key to success. The higher stages of the tide have been producing the better water quality, particularly across the last couple of hours of the run-in and the first two hours of the run-out. Surface lures and shallow-diving hardbodies have been producing the better-quality fish.

Southport

Offshore fishing has been producing plenty of smiles, with snapper, cobia, tuna, tuskfish and some quality tailor featuring prominently in catches.

Inshore, flathead remain the main target throughout the Broadwater. The bite has been a little inconsistent from day to day, with anglers needing to stay mobile and experiment with different lure styles and presentations to find what the fish want.

Summer whiting have also started to show up in the Broadwater, a welcome sign that the warmer months are beginning to take hold.

Tweed Coast

Before the northerlies arrived and kept boats tied to the ramp later in the week, the 24 and 30-fathom grounds were producing quality snapper, pearl perch, flathead, Moses perch and kingfish.

Those who managed to fish the early-week weather window found a reliable mixed bag across the offshore reefs, with kingfish adding a bit of extra excitement amongst the usual bottom species.

Fishing has been a little weather dependent this week, but there are still some quality fish being caught for those finding a window.

The upper reaches of the Tweed system are producing the better reports, with the warmer conditions really starting to bring the estuaries to life. Around Boyds Bay Bridge, mangrove jacks have fired up, with several anglers being smoked on live baits around the structure.

Offshore opportunities have been limited, but when boats have managed to get out, kingfish, cobia, snapper and pearl perch have all been reported. The kingfish have been particularly impressive on the inshore reefs, while some of the cobia being landed are absolute monsters, making heavy tackle a wise choice.

Conditions on the Tweed Bar have generally been reasonable, although anglers are reminded to check conditions carefully before heading offshore.

Ballina

Conditions look fairly rough for offshore anglers until at least Sunday, limiting opportunities to get out wide. The few boats that have managed to fish the close reefs have reported snapper, trag and a handful of mulloway.

In the Richmond River, the better bream fishing has been from the ferry through to Broadwater, with mullet fillets, pilchards and prawns all producing fish.

Flathead fishing has been a little inconsistent due to the fluctuating weather. Some of the better quality fish have come from the lower reaches, while the larger numbers have been coming from upriver around the ferry and through North Creek.

The run-in tides have produced a few whiting, with yabbies and worms proving the most effective baits.

Mud crab catches have been improving, although most reports are coming from well upstream in the various arms of the river.

Strong winds have made beach fishing difficult for much of the week, but conditions should improve later in the weekend and make the beaches worth a look.

Evans Head

Airforce Beach continues to produce the odd tailor, along with bream, flathead and whiting.

Around the north and south breakwalls, anglers are finding bream, flathead and school jewfish, while a few luderick are still hanging around.

Further upstream, flathead and whiting are fishing well. There have also been reports of what may have been mangrove jacks starting to show up as water temperatures rise. Mud crabs are becoming more active in the upper reaches as well.

Offshore, the close reefs are producing snapper and trag, while the wider grounds continue to turn up pearl perch. Longtail tuna and yellowfin tuna are also being reported offshore, along with the occasional kingfish and cobia.

Brunswick Heads

Deep-sea fishing has been a little quieter this week, with persistent north-easterly winds limiting opportunities offshore. The boats that have managed to get out have reported a few jewfish from the local grounds, although catches have otherwise been fairly modest.

Beach and rock anglers are finding dart, jewfish and bream, while the Brunswick River is producing plenty of bream, blackfish and flathead.

There are also a few legal-sized mud crabs amongst the many undersized crabs currently being encountered throughout the system.

Yamba

Fishing around Yamba has been a little quieter this week, with the exceptionally clean water making conditions tougher in the lower river. The better reports continue to come from further upstream, where anglers are finding more consistent fishing.

There are still flathead, whiting and a few school jewfish being caught throughout the river system, although catches have been a little patchy. Offshore reports have been limited, but conditions have generally been good enough for anglers to find snapper on the local reefs.

Mud crabs continue to be caught throughout the system, and there is some optimism that improving conditions could lead to a better blue swimmer crab season after several lean years.

The blackfish season has now largely wrapped up as water temperatures increase and summer species begin to take over.

FRESHWATER FISHING

Ewan Maddock Dam

Some nice bass are being caught on purple hardbody lures.

North Pine Dam

North Pine is fishing extremely well, with bass schooling and moving regularly around the deeper water and points. One recent session produced around 20 fish in just over an hour.

Soft plastics rigged with jig spinners and metal spoons remain the standout presentations. Cast long across the points, allow the lure to sink, then work it back with a mixture of slow rolling, hopping and pauses to cover the water column.

North Pine Dam has sen anglers needing to locate the larger schools of bass to find consistent action.

Lake Kurwongbah

Lake Kurwongbah continues to fish well, with plenty of bass being caught, particularly during the early morning and late afternoon periods.

Wivenhoe Dam

Wivenhoe is fishing well, with bass holding through the main basin along the old river channel. Anglers with sounders should focus on locating schools along the river bends before working them with metal spoons and soft plastics on heavier jigheads.

There are also plenty of yellowbelly around the edges. Areas such as Platypus Cliffs and the shoreline structure near the basin are worth targeting with TN60-style lipless crankbaits, crankbaits and metal blades.

Hinze Dam

Hinze remains one of the standout bass fisheries in South East Queensland. Fish are beginning to congregate along the major points leading into the main basin from both the eastern and western arms.

Crankbaits, lipless crankbaits, chatterbaits and 3-inch paddle-tail soft plastics on half-ounce jigheads are all accounting for fish. Saratoga are also active, with early morning sessions around the backs of bays producing fish on surface lures and spinnerbaits.

Moogerah Dam

Moogerah continues to fish exceptionally well, with bass now holding much shallower as water temperatures continue to rise. Many fish are sitting in 15-25ft of water around the timbered edges, making them ideal targets for reaction-style lures. Chatterbaits, crankbaits, lipless crankbaits, soft plastics and spinnerbaits are all producing fish.
