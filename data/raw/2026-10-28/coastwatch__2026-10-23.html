Fishing Report for 23 October 2026

Fishing Report Thu 22 Oct 2026

Hervey Bay / Fraser Island

Weather has again been the biggest talking point across the region, with strong winds and rain limiting opportunities for both offshore and bay anglers. Despite that, mackerel continue to headline catches, with broadbar and school mackerel showing up throughout Hervey Bay. Around the Urangan Pier, anglers are still finding school mackerel, queenfish, giant trevally and golden trevally, while the beach end of the pier is producing whiting and the occasional flathead.

The Great Sandy Straits continues to fish well for both winter and summer whiting, particularly across the flats and around the creek mouths. Flathead remain widespread, while grunter are coming from the channels and river systems. Threadfin salmon, blue salmon and barramundi are all active through the straits, the Mary River system and local creeks. The Burrum system remains one of the standout options for mangrove jacks, with quality grunter and whiting also featuring in catches.

Offshore, coral trout are biting well on the shallow reefs, while anglers who can avoid the sharks are still bringing home grassy sweetlip and other reef species. Spanish mackerel continue to be reported around Sandy Cape Shoals, while the wider grounds are steadily improving with black marlin, striped marlin and yellowfin tuna all starting to feature in catches. Tuna numbers in the bay itself remain patchy.

Fraser Island reports remain mixed. Some anglers are finding a few tailor, while others are struggling, although whiting, dart and the occasional trevally continue to keep beach anglers interested. Sand crabs remain a better option than mud crabs at present, while patches of nuisance “snot weed” throughout parts of Platypus Bay and the central bay are affecting both fishing and boating activities. Recent rainfall should also improve prospects for mud crabs over the coming weeks.

Tin Can Bay

It’s looking like a good weekend to be a duck, with plenty of rain in the forecast. The upside is that the wet weather should get the crabs on the move, making it well worth setting a few pots.

For anglers keen to brave the conditions, the best option will be to tuck up a sheltered creek and chase mangrove jacks, cod and bream. There may even be the chance of finding one of the elusive stocked Tin Can Bay barramundi.

For everyone else, it might be the perfect weekend to tidy the tackle box, respool a few reels or give the trailer bearings some overdue attention.

Noosa

No report this week due to inclement weather.

Offshore opportunities have been limited this week, but a good weather window today produced some quality fish around Double Island Point. Cod, amberjack and pearl perch all featured in catches, with anglers making the most of the improving conditions.

North Reef continues to produce quality pearl perch and snapper for anglers making the most of the weather windows.

Further offshore, the Barwon Banks are fishing well for goldband snapper, Moses perch, tuskfish and pearl perch, with good numbers of fish being reported across the wider grounds.

Some quality flathead were still being caught prior to the recent rain event, with several larger fish reported throughout the system.

The river may take a few days to settle as the fresh water works through, but once conditions stabilise it is expected to fish exceptionally well, particularly for anglers targeting the estuary species that typically respond to a good flush.

Maroochydore

Jewfish at the cod hole and flathead at the mouth of Eudlo and Petrie Creeks.

Mooloolaba

With widespread rain and dirty water throughout the region, crabbing may be the best option over the weekend. The deeper holes in the Mooloolah River are worth a look for those chasing a few early-season mud crabs.

For anglers keen to wet a line, the high tide around the river mouths should provide the best opportunity, with flathead and whiting the most likely targets.

Most of the local dams are expected to be closed due to rising water levels and inflows. Looking ahead, if conditions improve next week, the close inshore reefs should be worth exploring for grassy sweetlip on yakkas and squid. The dirty water offshore may also attract pelagic species such as dolphinfish and wahoo, with areas out to the East Cardinal Marker and Murphy’s Reef worth keeping an eye on.

Caloundra / Pumicestone Passage

The Sunshine Coast has copped a significant dose of wind and rain this week, with local waterways carrying plenty of runoff.

Despite the challenging weather, the inshore reefs have been producing a quality mixed bag including legal-sized tuskfish, grunter, parrotfish, snapper, sweetlip and mowong, whenever conditions have allowed.

For those waiting for conditions to settle, the good news is that a decent flush often sets up some excellent fishing on the inshore reefs. Once the weather eases, snapper, sweetlip and other reef species should be well worth targeting around the local grounds.

Before the dirty water arrived, Pumicestone Passage was still producing quality whiting and flathead, while the first signs of the summer fishery were beginning to emerge. Mangrove jacks had started showing up in the creeks and canal systems, and there were also reports of jewfish from the beaches and headlands.

Expect jewfish to be present in response to the freshwater in the system. Mud crabs are also expected to be one of the major beneficiaries of the recent rainfall.

No report with inclement weather. Charters resuming.

Northern Moreton Bay

The recent rain has pushed many anglers away from the creeks and into crabbing mode, with the mouths of the Pine River, Caboolture River and many of the Pumicestone Passage creeks expected to produce good mud crab catches.

Despite the weather, some quality snapper are still being caught around Scarborough, Mud Island and the mouth of the Brisbane River. In the northern bay, anglers are finding mack tuna and longtail tuna, while a few school mackerel continue to show up around the beacons.

Offshore, Spanish mackerel are being caught around Hutchies by anglers slow-trolling dead baits. A few dolphinfish are also being reported around the FADs, while Deep Tempest continues to produce quality snapper and pearl perch

Moreton Bay Offshore

Reports resume next week.

Redcliffe Peninsula

Bream, yellowfin whiting, flathead and the odd jewfish are coming from the Pine River, local jetties and around the Newport canal entrance.

Yellowfin whiting and flathead are also being caught along the Scarborough beaches and Redcliffe Spit.

Jewfish and snapper are being caught from the jetties, rocky points, beaches and the inshore reefs, while grunter are showing up around the inshore reefs and canal mouths.

Mangrove jacks continue to be reported from the canals and throughout the Pine River system.

Queenfish are also showing up on the flats around Deception Bay, while sand crabs are being caught in Bramble Bay.

Brisbane

Another fairly quiet week around Brisbane, with the recent weather continuing to limit opportunities for many anglers.

The Brisbane River is still producing school jewfish and grunter through the lower reaches, along with the occasional snapper and flathead. A few sand crabs are also being reported around Wellington Point, King Island and Green Island.

Most of the activity has been centred further upstream in the Pimpama, Coomera and Nerang systems, where anglers chasing whiting have continued to find a few fish despite the unsettled conditions. With plenty of fresh water still moving through local waterways, reports have been a little patchy and many anglers have simply stayed off the water altogether.

Wynnum / Manly

Very limited reports this week with the weather, with bream the main species being caught in the area.

Moreton Bay / Jumpinpin

Moreton Bay / Southern Moreton Bay

Weather has kept most boats off the bay over the past week, but anglers seeking shelter in the Brisbane River have still found some quality fish. Threadfin salmon have been the standout, holding along the deeper drop-offs and ledges. Soft vibes around the 95-110mm mark have been effective, while prawn-style soft plastics are also accounting for plenty of fish.

Jumpinpin

Before the recent fresh, Jumpinpin was producing good numbers of flathead around the Jacobs Well Channel, Bedrooms and Tipplers. Anglers were finding the better fish around bait schools and along the weed edges, with smaller 3-inch paddle-tail plastics proving particularly effective. A few quality giant trevally were also encountered around the Pin Bar, including fish into the 70-80cm class.

School mulloway were present throughout the system, with fish generally in the 60-70cm range coming from areas including the Pin Bar and nearby deeper holes. Threadfin salmon were also active around the Southern Bay Islands, Logan River mouth, Jacobs Well and Cabbage Tree Point, particularly along the deeper drop-offs adjacent to mangrove-lined banks.

Recent rainfall may have altered conditions considerably, but prior to the fresh there were still plenty of quality fish spread throughout the southern bay systems.

South Stradbroke Island

Gold Coast Broadwater

Challenging conditions again this weekend. Only a handful of people went out this week, with mixed results.

Depending on how much rain we get over the end of the working week will determine what to fish for. At the moment, the water clarity is not too bad, making it perfect for whiting on the run-in tide. The Nerang River still holds good numbers from the Council Chambers down to the Broadwater. If the rain continues, they may get pushed out to the main banks around Wave Break and Curlew Island.

Flathead continue to be caught around the Measured Mile, Tipplers Passage and the Aldershots. They are not in great numbers, but there are still a handful of fish in the 40-55cm range being reported.

Sand crabs have been around in good numbers in the Nerang River, around Crab Island and at Paradise Point. If the rain continues, it may slow them up and it may be better to target mud crabs in the deeper holes through the middle and lower sections of the Pimpama and Coomera rivers.

If we get a bit of a flush-out, fishing for jewfish in the Seaway should be a great option. These fish respond well to that first push of freshwater, especially around the start of the run-out tide.

All in all, not the best weekend to head out.

Southport

Most of the week has been spent either on land or fishing inshore waters, although one offshore charter managed to sneak out and find cobia, tuskfish, pearl perch, Moses perch and reef flathead. Sharks were a constant problem, however, making life difficult no matter where the boat moved.

Inshore, a few keen anglers persisted through the rain and were rewarded with whiting and flathead. The recent freshwater should shake things up and may actually improve the fishing, with whiting expected to fire in the Broadwater and more of the larger flathead likely to move towards the deeper bar entrances as the rivers continue to flush.

Tweed Coast

Fished close-in offshore mid week due to conditions, taking tuskfish and maori cod on the reefs, and a few bonito on the troll.

Despite another weather-affected week, anglers finding a break in the conditions are still encountering some quality fish.

Offshore, kingfish and cobia remain the standout species, with good numbers holding from the 18-fathom line right through to the wider grounds. Some of the cobia and kingfish being encountered are genuine trophy fish, and anglers are advised to fish appropriately heavy tackle. Further offshore, pearl perch are being caught in 100-150m of water, with snapper mixed through the catches.

A few isolated mackerel have also started to appear, although numbers remain patchy at this stage.

In the estuaries, the recent influx of fresh water has concentrated fish around drains and run-off areas. Trevally and mangrove jacks are feeding well in these zones, while larger flathead are beginning to show through the deeper sections of the system.

Ballina

The weather has made fishing tough this week, with very limited offshore opportunities and the Richmond River carrying plenty of dirty water. Offshore reports have been almost non-existent, with only a handful of boats managing to get out.

Before the recent runoff arrived, there were still a few flathead and bream being caught through the river system, along with some school mulloway. As conditions settle, the breakwalls and areas behind the CBD could be worth a look for mulloway on lures.

Further upstream, anglers may find some better fishing in the creeks and tributaries, with bass likely to respond to surface lures, frogs and bug-style presentations once conditions improve. Crabbing should also be worth keeping an eye on over the coming days as the fresh water begins to move through the system.

Evans Head

Airforce Beach and Main Beach are producing the odd jewfish and bream.

Around the river walls at the mouth, anglers are finding jewfish and bream, while the lower reaches of the Evans River are producing bream, flathead and whiting.

A few quality mud crabs are also being reported throughout the system.

Offshore activity has been very limited this week due to the weather.

Brunswick Heads

Once again, the weather has not been kind to deep-sea anglers, with very few opportunities to get offshore this week.

Beach and rock fishing, however, has finally fired up. Tailor and jewfish have been the standout catches, with bream and dart also featuring regularly. The abundance of baitfish around the beaches and headlands is certainly helping the cause.

The Brunswick River is producing bream, flathead and mangrove jacks, while the recent rain has mud crabs on the move and a few quality muddies are being reported throughout the system.

Yamba

Fishing around Yamba remains much the same as last week, with most of the better reports continuing to come from the upper reaches of the system. There have been good flathead reported in the lower reaches. Despite recent rainfall, the river remains relatively clear, although that could change if more water comes down from further upstream.

The better fishing continues to be around Maclean, Harwood and Lawrence, where anglers are still finding blackfish in good numbers. Should the river colour up over the coming week, there is potential for these fish to push further downstream.

Blue swimmer crabs have been present up until the recent rainfall. Increased freshwater flow should also help stir up mud crab activity throughout the system.

FRESHWATER FISHING

With the current weather event and localised flooding there have been dam closures so please check SEQ Water before heading out.

Wivenhoe Dam

Wivenhoe remains open and is continuing to fish well. Bass and golden perch are being found along the old riverbed and around the rocky banks and cliff faces throughout the main basin.

Soft plastics, metal spoons, lipless crankbaits, chatterbaits and blades are all worth a look, with fish holding both along the edges and in deeper water.

Hinze Dam

With many impoundments receiving significant inflows, anglers are advised to check dam access before travelling, as some facilities may be closed due to rising water levels. One dam that remains open and is fishing particularly well is Hinze Dam.

The recent rain has been a positive for both bass and saratoga. Saratoga are responding well to small spinnerbaits, surface lures and other presentations worked high in the water column, particularly through the upper reaches of the dam where fish are actively feeding around freshly inundated areas.

Bass are also making the most of the conditions, holding around timber and submerged structure throughout the system. Spinnerbaits, chatterbaits and crankbaits that provide a bit of flash and vibration are proving effective in the dirtier water, with anglers finding fish around snags, standing timber and points.

Moogerah Dam

Moogerah has received plenty of inflow but remains a worthwhile option for freshwater anglers. As fresh water pushes into the system, fish are moving upstream into the feeder creeks looking for food.

Chatterbaits, crankbaits and spinnerbaits are all producing results around structure, while anglers prepared to move further up the system are finding bass and golden perch actively feeding around the inflows.
