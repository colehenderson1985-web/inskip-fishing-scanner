SEQ Fishing Reports

This report is compiled every Thursday afternoon. If you see a report that interests you, call the info source for more detailed info and bait, tackle and hire boat availability.

Fishing Report for 16 October 2026

Hervey Bay / Fraser Island

Mackerel remain the headline act across the region, with broadbar and school mackerel dominating catches around the Urangan Pier, local beaches and the wider bay. The pier is also producing quality queenfish, giant trevally, golden trevally and the occasional Spanish mackerel, while whiting continue to be caught along the Hervey Bay foreshores and throughout the Great Sandy Straits. 

The Great Sandy Straits and local river systems are fishing well for grunter, flathead, threadfin salmon, blue salmon and barramundi. Mangrove jacks are firing throughout the western creeks and the Burrum system, while quality whiting are also featuring in the middle reaches. 

Offshore, coral trout, grassy sweetlip, cod and Spanish mackerel continue to feature, while billfish activity is building with juvenile black marlin, striped marlin and yellowfin tuna all being reported. Around Rooneys Point and Station Hill, the first consistent run of juvenile black marlin for the season has provided plenty of excitement for gamefishers.

Fraser Island’s tailor season remains patchy depending on location and conditions, although quality greenbacks are still being caught through the central sector. Anglers are also finding dart, whiting and the occasional trevally along the surf beaches. Boaties should remain aware of lingering whale traffic and patches of nuisance “snot weed” in parts of Platypus Bay and the central bay.

Tin Can Bay

Wet and windy conditions are expected this weekend, so anglers will be best served heading up the creeks to escape the worst of the showers and south-easterly winds.

With the overcast conditions, mangrove jacks should be high on the target list. Surface lures such as frogs and 70-90mm poppers worked tight against snaggy structure are worth a look, while live baits and fresh strips of mullet continue to produce fish. Even when the jacks are quiet, there are usually plenty of cod and bream around to keep rods bending.

A few stocked Tin Can Bay barramundi have also been caught over the past couple of weeks as water temperatures continue to rise. Live baits have accounted for some captures, while slowly worked soft plastic prawns remain a reliable option.

Crabbing has been a little quiet, although the recent rainfall should help get the crabs moving. It’s still worth setting a few pots, with fresh baits and a longer soak generally producing the best results.

Noosa

There are still some quality flathead being caught through the lower reaches of the Noosa River, with a number of better fish coming from the deeper channels and drop-offs.

Bream and whiting remain the bread-and-butter species throughout the system, providing consistent fishing for both bait and lure anglers. A few tailor have also been taken by land-based anglers in the Noosaville reaches over the past week.

Not a huge amount to report over the last week. The closer offshore reefs were fishing well, with North Reef producing a reliable mix of snapper and sweetlip while further afield, Sunshine Reef was yielding quality pearl perch, while Double Island Point produced hard-fighting amberjack along with plenty of snapper.

Plenty of fish being caught with The Hards, Double Island Point and North Reef all producing quality catches.

A wide range of reef species are on the chew, making this one of the most productive times of the year to head offshore. With conditions and water temperatures aligning, both this month and next are shaping up as prime months for reef fishing, with anglers finding quality fish across both the close and wider grounds.

The lower estuary continues to produce some quality flathead, while a number of belter bream have also been reported throughout the system.

A few smaller mulloway are being caught in the ski run areas, with soft plastic frog-style lures proving effective on the jewies.

Maroochydore

Fishing activity has been relatively quiet this week, although anglers are still finding a few quality fish throughout the system.

Bream and flathead have been reported around the mouths of both Eudlo Creek and Petrie Creek, while a handful of whiting continue to be caught around Chambers Island.

Mooloolaba

It has been a quieter week than usual on the Sunshine Coast, with fishing reports fairly limited across both offshore and freshwater systems.

The Maroochy River is producing a few flathead around Bli Bli, while quality whiting continue to come from around Chambers Island. In the Mooloolah River, anglers are finding bream around La Balsa Park, with the trawler area producing the odd trevally. A few mangrove jacks are also being caught in the Kawana canal systems as water temperatures continue to rise.

With live worms proving effective, estuary anglers are still managing a reasonable mixed bag despite the slower conditions. 

Caloundra / Pumicestone Passage

Persistent wind and rain have limited fishing opportunities this week.

Those who have managed to get out are still finding whiting and flathead throughout Pumicestone Passage, while mud crabs continue to be reported from the local creek and canal systems.

Northern Moreton Bay

With a wet and windy weekend forecast, anglers may be better off heading into the upper reaches of the Pine and Caboolture river systems. Flathead remain a good option in these areas, while the odd jewfish and mangrove jack are also being caught.

For those targeting jacks, the key is to fish tight to structure, use heavier leader and be prepared to lock up quickly once hooked.

Brisbane

Weather has once again limited opportunities across much of the bay and river system, resulting in a quieter week of reports. For those finding sheltered water, the Brisbane River is still producing the odd school jewfish and grunter, while flathead continue to show up around the lower reaches and river mouth structure.

Whiting remain a possibility through the bay-side creeks and flats, although conditions have made consistent fishing difficult. A few sweetlip are still being caught around the shallower island grounds, while both sand and mud crabs remain on the move as water temperatures continue to rise. With poor weather forecast to continue through the weekend, protected river and creek systems are likely to offer the best chance of putting together a mixed bag.

Wynnum / Manly

Fishing activity has largely been concentrated around the sheltered estuaries this week, with windy conditions limiting opportunities elsewhere.

The mouths of Lota Creek and Tingalpa Creek are producing whiting and flathead, while mud crab numbers continue to improve throughout the local estuary systems. With conditions remaining breezy, these protected areas are proving the most reliable option for anglers and crabbers alike.

Jumpinpin

There are still some quality flathead being caught throughout the southern end of the system at Cobby Cobby and Jumpipin bar. The shallow lagoons and flats on South Stradbroke Island have been producing some larger fish, while a few nice specimens have been taken at Jacobs Well jetty.

A few mud crabs are also being caught in the Logan River, while tailor remain scattered along the South Stradbroke Island beaches.

Slower week for flathead reports, but some quality threadfin salmon, whiting, trevally and tailor — and a few mulloway at Jumpinpin bar.

Gold Coast Broadwater

Fairly ordinary conditions are forecast this weekend, with showers and consistent winds across both days. The beaches and main channels in the Broadwater will be pretty wild, so it’s best to avoid those areas.

If you’re still keen and have a boat with some shelter from the showers, there are a few whiting in the Nerang River. The best time at the moment is the night-time run-in tide, with fish being caught near Sundale Bridge and the Council Chambers. They are also being caught in the Pimpama River near the mouth and in the North Arm of the Coomera River.

If you’re out there, it could be worth putting out a few pots, with some sand crabs and the odd mud crab being caught. Another option in these conditions is to head into the canals and chase bream, trevally, cod and the occasional mangrove jack.

Not the best weekend to head out, so it might be one for getting the boat or fishing gear in order.

Southport

Weather has limited offshore opportunities this week, with only a couple of half-day trips making it onto the water. Those who did manage to fish offshore found tuna, tuskfish, reef flathead and tailor, along with a few snapper, although snapper numbers appear to have eased slightly compared to recent weeks.

In the estuaries, conditions have been far more productive. Recent full-day Broadwater sessions produced excellent whiting fishing, along with bream, slatey bream, flounder and flathead. The quality whiting action was a particular highlight, with the species continuing to feature strongly throughout the southern bay systems.

Tweed Coast

No report this week.

Offshore, there are good numbers of kingfish for those able to get out, and anglers don’t have to venture too far wide to find them. Some sizeable cobia are also being encountered around the 50-metre line, while excellent pearl perch continue to provide consistent action on the wider grounds. Snapper have been mixed in amongst the pearlies, although most fish have been on the smaller side.

A few early-season mackerel have also started to appear, albeit in isolated catches, while the occasional quality wahoo has been reported offshore. Beach fishing, meanwhile, has been largely blown out by the weather.

In the estuaries, the better fishing has been further upstream towards the freshwater reaches near the weirs, where mangrove jacks have become increasingly active during the warmer, humid evenings. Closer to the river mouth and around the bridge systems, the usual mix of estuary species continues to keep anglers busy.

Ballina

Report pending.

Evans Head

Airforce Beach is producing the tailor, along with the occasional jewfish and a handful of bream. Around the north and south breakwalls, anglers are picking up bream, flathead and whiting, while a few jewfish have been reported during the evening periods.

Further upstream, mud crab numbers appear to be improving again, while trevally, grunter and flathead are all featuring in catches throughout the river system.

Offshore, the close reefs are producing snapper and trag, while the wider grounds continue to turn up pearl perch. The odd yellowfin tuna and kingfish are also still being reported when conditions allow.

Brunswick Heads

Offshore fishing has been effectively shut down this week, with persistent swell and strong winds preventing most boats from heading to sea.

Beach and rockwall anglers are still finding a few fish, with dart, bream and jewfish making up the bulk of catches. In the Brunswick River, the warmer conditions are starting to kick the estuary fishery into gear, with mangrove jacks showing up around the harbour and along the rock walls. Mud crabs are also being caught throughout the system, although many of the crabs being checked remain undersized, with only a handful of legal keepers reported.

Yamba

Offshore reports have been virtually non-existent this week, with very few boats managing to head to sea.

The estuary remains the best option, with the cleaner water conditions continuing to favour the upper reaches of the system. Anglers targeting flathead are still finding the better fishing further upstream around Harwood, Lawrence and the surrounding reaches. There have also been reports of the odd mangrove jack starting to show up as water temperatures slowly rise.

FRESHWATER FISHING

North Pine Dam

North Pine continues to fish very well, although anglers fishing from the bank may need to put in a little more effort to locate fish. The Bullocky’s Rest and Forgans Cove areas are both producing bass, particularly for anglers working heavy soft plastics and metal spoons through deeper water. Early mornings remain productive around structure with spinnerbaits, however the better bite has generally been occurring later in the afternoon. Fishing from around 2pm through to dark is producing some quality bass, with metal spoons, spinnerbaits and soft plastics all proving effective.

Lake Kurwongbah

Lake Kurwongbah continues to produce some good bass, with anglers reporting success trolling soft plastics.

The South Pine River and associated creeks are also fishing well for bass, particularly during the afternoon bite period, making them a worthwhile option for anglers looking to avoid the worst of the weather.

Wivenhoe Dam

Wivenhoe is producing some excellent-quality bass at the moment. Anglers are finding success following the old riverbed through the main basin and targeting schools with soft plastics, metal spoons and lipless crankbaits. Fish numbers have been strong and the average size has been particularly encouraging, making Wivenhoe one of the better bass options in the region this week.

Hinze Dam

Hinze remains one of the standout Southeast Queensland impoundments, with good numbers of quality bass being caught throughout the main basin. Fish are holding around points, submerged timber and structure extending from both the eastern and western arms. Small soft plastics rigged with jig spinners, chatterbaits and crankbaits are all producing fish, while trollers are finding success working diving lures that reach around 20ft. Saratoga are also becoming increasingly active, with small glidebaits, spinnerbaits and surface lures fished high in the water column accounting for fish around the backs of bays and timbered edges.

Moogerah Dam

Moogerah continues to fish exceptionally well, with plenty of bass and yellowbelly on the move throughout the dam. Fish are holding relatively shallow, generally in 15-25ft of water, making reaction-style lures the standout option. Spinnerbaits, chatterbaits, lipless crankbaits, crankbaits and soft plastics are all accounting for quality fish. The tree-lined banks, particularly through the Six Knot Zone, are holding large concentrations of fish and are well worth targeting. Yellowbelly are also showing up consistently, especially around the Gorge opposite the boat ramp where metal blades and lipless crankbaits are proving particularly effective.
