Fishing Report for 27 November 2026 Copy

Fishing Report Sat 28 Nov 2026

Please note the following fishing closures:

  • Spanner crab Southern Closure: 1 November to 15 December; Tidal waters of spanner crab managed areas A and B. See https://www.dpi.qld.gov.au/services/fisheries/closures/tidal-seasons for further information

Hervey Bay / Fraser Island

Conditions have continued to improve across the region, although another wet weekend is forecast. Around the Urangan Pier, the water remains a little stained but there are still plenty of herring holding around the pylons, attracting grunter, blue salmon, flathead and the occasional jewfish. Whiting have also been fishing well around the sandbars and beach end of the pier, while a few pencil squid have started to appear around the cleaner stages of the tide.

Along the town beaches, whiting and grunter remain the standout species and should continue to fish well around the moon phases, while creek mouths are producing flathead and blue salmon. Mud crabbing has been productive following the floods, with good numbers of crabs spread through the system, while sand crabs have pushed into the shallower waters off Urangan and the southern bay.

On the inshore reefs, coral trout, grassy sweetlip, grunter and cod continue to dominate catches. The pelagic scene is also building momentum, with mack tuna widespread throughout the bay, increasing numbers of longtail tuna and the first larger spotted mackerel starting to show. Offshore, game fishing remains excellent, with black, blue and striped marlin, yellowfin tuna and large dolphinfish all featuring strongly in recent catches. Wahoo, cobia and Spanish mackerel are also being reported from the shoal grounds.

Fraser Island is producing quality tailor north of Happy Valley, along with dart and the odd flathead. However, anglers should be aware that snot weed has moved into many of the surf gutters and is impacting beach fishing in some areas.

Tin Can Bay

The forecast suggests it could be a wet weekend, but the strong run-in tides should have the local creek systems fishing well.

Mangrove jacks are worth targeting on the morning flood tides, with anglers casting lures deep into the snags before the midday high finding the best opportunities. Grunter remain in good numbers through the deeper creek holes and around rubble beds, with fresh squid strips, soft vibes around 80mm, 3.5-inch curl-tail plastics and small prawn imitations all accounting for fish. Mixed amongst the grunter have been plenty of tarpon, giant trevally and golden trevally, making for plenty of action on light tackle.

Mud crabs should also be on the move with the building tides, making it well worth setting a few pots for a long soak over the weekend.

The whiting fishery continues to improve, with fish in the high-30cm to low-40cm range now being caught consistently. Early morning run-in tides remain the prime time to fish yabbies or beach worms across the local banks and flats.

There have also been a few mackerel showing up in the bay over the past couple of weeks. Anglers trolling spoons or small diving lures around Inskip Point and Big Mick are finding the odd fish.

Noosa

The Noosa River has continued to fish well as conditions have held off and the water retains its blue. Bream remain the standout species through the lower reaches, while trevally are being taken in Woods Bay.

Recent sessions around the lower Tewantin reach and marina area have produced a few quality flathead, while upstream past the ferry is producing grunter bream.

Mud crabbing has been fairly steady, with a few mud crabs continuing to come from the Tewantin area and adjoining lower reaches, although catches have been modest rather than spectacular.

Double Island Point continues to produce some quality reef fishing, with goldband snapper, amberjack, snapper and mulloway featuring prominently in recent catches.

Closer to Noosa, both Sunshine Reef and North Reef are turning up pearl perch, grassy sweetlip, Moses perch and tuskfish, providing anglers with a reliable mixed bag across the local grounds.

The Hards and Barwon Banks continue to produce quality reef fishing, with pearl perch, Moses perch, goldband snapper and tuskfish all featuring in recent catches.

Strong current remains a factor on the wider grounds, but conditions are still very fishable and anglers prepared to work around the run are being rewarded with a quality mixed bag.

Charters resume in December.

Maroochydore

It has been a fairly quiet week around the Maroochy River, although there are still a few fish being caught.

Bream and whiting are being reported around Chambers Island, while mangrove jacks are showing up north of the Bli Bli Bridge. A few mud crabs are also being caught upstream of Chambers Island.

Mooloolaba

Mangrove jacks are becoming a more common capture throughout the local estuaries, with fish being caught around snags, bridge structure, pontoons and other hard cover in both the Mooloolah and Maroochy river systems.

Flathead are also on the move, with many fish appearing to push back upstream after their spawning period. Offshore, pearl perch are coming from the wider grounds off Currimundi and Caloundra.

Dolphinfish are showing up around the FADs, including some quality bulls in the 15-20kg range. Blue marlin have been reported from the wider offshore grounds, while black marlin are being encountered around the trench off Moreton Island and closer inshore around the Mooloolaba 8 Mile.

Pelagic activity remains fairly patchy overall, although there are a few school mackerel following bait schools and the odd Spanish mackerel beginning to appear. A couple of barramundi have also been reported recently from the Maroochy River, with warming water temperatures appearing to increase fish activity.

Caloundra / Pumicestone Passage

Whiting and flathead are being caught around the mouths of Bells Creek and Coochin Creek, while mangrove jacks are showing up throughout the Pelican Waters canal system.

Inside the Caloundra Bar, anglers are finding grunter, trevally and queenfish. Offshore, the local coffee rock grounds are producing squire and sweetlip, while the Caloundra 5 Mile is turning up school mackerel.

Further offshore, the Caloundra 12 Mile is fishing well for pearl perch and tuskfish. Mud crabs are also being reported throughout the local creek systems.

Report pending.

Northern Moreton Bay

Offshore fishing has been pretty good, with the first of the summer pelagics beginning to appear. There have been reports of mackerel and wahoo from the wider grounds, although weather conditions have limited opportunities for anglers to get out wide

Closer in, the 40-60 metre grounds are producing good numbers of pearl perch over hard bottom, along with snapper and other reef species. Within Moreton Bay things are transitional with pelagic activity is starting to build, with school mackerel around the beacons, particularly the Four Beacons area, while mack tuna are widespread and the odd longtail tuna and spotted mackerel have also been reported.

The deeper marks around Pearl Channel and Harry Atkinson Reef are producing jewfish and larger snapper, although sharks are making life difficult for anglers. On the shallower grounds, whiting, grunter and grassy sweetlip are all contributing to quality mixed bags.

The estuaries are fishing particularly well following the earlier rain. The Pine and Caboolture systems are holding plenty of bait, with bream, whiting and flathead all featuring in catches. Anglers fishing early and late around structure are also finding mangrove jacks and threadfin salmon. Mud crabs remain active through the middle and upper reaches of both river systems.

Freshwater

Lake Kurwongbah and Lake Samsonvale are both fishing very well. Fish are schooling around many of the points, while surface lures are producing bass and saratoga during the early morning and late afternoon periods. At North Pine, deeper water fishing with trolled hardbodies and soft plastics continues to be productive.

The local freshwater streams are also looking healthy after the recent rain — including 4 Mile Creek — with the North Pine, South Pine and upper Caboolture systems all worth a look for bass anglers.

Moreton Bay Offshore

Dolphinfish continue to provide exciting offshore action around the FADs off Moreton Island, with anglers finding fish whenever conditions allow them to get offshore.

The reefs are also producing a reliable mixed bag, including snapper, pearl perch and reef flathead.

Redcliffe Peninsula

Bream, yellowfin whiting, flathead and the odd jewfish are being caught throughout the Pine River, around local jetties and near the Newport canal entrance.

Yellowfin whiting and flathead are also coming from the Scarborough beaches and the Redcliffe Spit, while jewfish and snapper continue to be caught from the jetties, rocky points, beaches and inshore reefs.

Grunter are being reported from the inshore reefs, canal mouths, Woody Point Jetty, Hayes Inlet and throughout the Pine River system. Grassy sweetlip are also featuring in catches from the inshore reefs and around Woody Point Jetty.

The odd dart is beginning to show along Margate Beach, while mangrove jacks continue to be caught in the canals and throughout the Pine River.

Queenfish and trevally are active across the flats of both Deception Bay and Bramble Bay. School mackerel and cobia are also being reported from Bramble Bay.

Sand crabs remain plentiful in Bramble Bay, with a few mud crabs also being caught in the upper reaches of local creeks.

Brisbane

The weather continues to make fishing difficult, with most local anglers waiting for a break in conditions.

For those prepared to brave it, the Brisbane River remains the best option, with jewfish, bream and flathead still coming from the lower reaches and river-mouth areas.

Wynnum / Manly

School mackerel are being caught around Green Island by anglers trolling.

Sand crabs remain plentiful in the shallows along the foreshore and around the bay islands, while sweetlip and squire are being caught around Green Island and the Wellington Point. A few mud crabs are also being reported in Tingalpa Creek.

Moreton Bay / Jumpinpin

Moreton Bay / Southern Moreton Bay

Moreton Bay has been fishing well across both the shallow and deeper grounds. Around Harry Atkinson Reef, particularly the Pipeline area, anglers are finding quality snapper in the 60-70cm range. Four-inch paddle-tail plastics have been particularly effective on these larger fish.

The deeper sections of the bay, including the Rainbow Channel, wrecks and surrounding ledges, are producing pan-sized snapper, sweetlip, spangled emperor and the occasional golden trevally.

On the shallow reefs, the north-western side of Peel Island and the southern end around Horseshoe Bay continue to produce good numbers of snapper and sweetlip. Goat Island has also been turning up some quality snapper, particularly along the eastern side.

Jumpinpin

Threadfin salmon remain active throughout the southern bay, with schools being found around Macleay Island, the Saltworks, Russell Island and Karragarra Island. School jewfish are also holding through these areas, with vibes and prawn-imitation lures proving effective.

Flathead have been a little quieter over the past fortnight, although fish in the 50-60cm range are still being caught. The better fishing has shifted further into the back sections around Cabbage Tree Point and Tabby Tabby Island.

Jewfish fishing has improved considerably, with good numbers being reported from Millionaires Row, Crusoe Island, Slipping Sands and the Jumpinpin Bar. Tailor schools have also moved into the system, with plenty of fish coming in on the run-in tide.

Trevally continue to show throughout the Cabbage Tree Point and Jacobs Well areas, while threadfin salmon remain active between the boat ramps at Cabbage Tree Point and Jacobs Well.

Jumpinpin

No report this week.

Trevally continue to fire throughout the system, providing some of the best action of the week.

A few mangrove jacks are also being caught around the Pin, with fish holding tight in the mangroves as water temperatures continue to rise. Flathead fishing has been steady, without really setting the world on fire.

There are plenty of smaller mulloway throughout the system at present, although reports of larger fish have been limited. Whiting have been the standout species, with multiple fish over 40cm reported.

A few tailor are still being caught, while threadfin salmon continue to show up in local catches.

South Stradbroke Island

It may be shaping up as a wet weekend, but there are still a few opportunities for anglers willing to work around the conditions.

Mangrove jacks are holding around the mangroves and pontoons in Saltwater Creek, while flathead are being caught in the Southern Branch of the Coomera River.

A few John Dory are also being reported from beneath the Ephraim Island Bridge.

Water clarity remains fairly murky following the recent rain, so natural baits are proving most effective. For lure anglers, adding plenty of scent to soft plastics is helping to improve results.

Gold Coast Broadwater

The whiting season is in full swing now with great catches coming in. With the bigger tides, the banks near Wave Break and Crab Island have been producing good numbers of fish. Further up the Nerang to the Council Chambers have also had good numbers. The Coomera and Pimpama rivers have also fished okay but not the same numbers as the Broadwater area.

It’s a great time to wade the shallows for those fishing land-based. Whiting definitely prefer a bit of run in the tide and can slow down across the change in tides. With the bigger high tide early afternoon, it’s a great time to put your pots out for a muddy. A couple of hours across the top of the tide right up in the mangrove shallows should produce a few.

The beaches are looking great for tailor as they make their way back south. Good deep water in close with low tide at the moment is the better option to get your baits and lures out to the back bank and whitewater.

A few bream and grunter were caught this week along South Stradbroke. The water clarity is not as clear as it has been, which probably explains the increase in numbers.

The current has been roaring around the Seaway with the big tides, but a few jew were caught early on the run-in tide.

Southport

Offshore anglers are finding a nice mixed bag of reef species, with paternoster rigs baited with pilchards and strips of yakka or slimey mackerel producing the best results.

Inshore fishing has been a little tougher, although some quality whiting are still being caught in the Pimpama River for anglers prepared to put in the time and work through the slower bite periods.

Tweed Coast

Report pending.

Offshore fishing continues to improve, with Spanish and spotted mackerel beginning to show up in closer alongside a few wahoo. The wahoo are out a bit further where some yellowfin tuna have also been picked up. Dolphinfish are also being reported at the FADS, while snapper and pearl perch remain reliable on the broken reef in 35-45m of water. While the fish are fairly scattered, anglers locating them are finding good numbers, with snapper generally ranging from 45-80cm.

In the estuaries, some strong trevally fishing continues, with fish to 85cm turning up throughout the Tweed as well as some good sized jewfish. Abundant flathead, and whiting fishing has also been excellent, with good catches both at the front end, and down past Chinderah. Strong catches on both bait and surface lures.

Mangrove jack continue to become more active in the canals, although most fish reported this week have been in the 30-40cm range. A few tailor are also being caught around the river mouth and beaches, generally in the 35-55cm bracket.

Ballina

Report pending.

Evans Head

Airforce Beach has been producing a few jewfish around the full moon, particularly during the evening, along with the occasional fish through the afternoon. Bream, flathead and whiting are also being caught along the beach.

Around the north and south breakwalls, anglers are finding jewfish and whiting, while one angler recently landed a decent cobia.

In the river, a few mangrove jacks are starting to show up with the warmer weather. Good mud crabs are still being caught in the upper reaches.

Offshore reports have been limited due to the weather, although anglers making it out have found snapper and trag around Chaos Reef.

Brunswick Heads

Offshore fishing has been fairly quiet, although anglers making it out have reported a few snapper and kingfish from the local grounds.

The beaches are producing bream, flathead and whiting, while jewfish remain a feature along the rock walls. In the Brunswick River, anglers are finding a good mixed bag of bream, mangrove jacks, flathead, whiting and jewfish. Tarwhine are also showing up throughout the system.

A few mud crabs are being reported, with the warmer weather helping keep the crabbers interested.

Yamba

The river continues to fish reasonably well despite still carrying a bit of colour. School jewfish remain the main attraction, although anglers are having to contend with plenty of sharks around the schools.

Offshore reports have been limited, but there are still snapper being caught on the closer reefs. There have been no reports of pelagic species yet.

Mud crabbing appears to be productive upriver, with large numbers of crab pots being worked around Maclean and the Cut Off. The level of effort suggests there are still good numbers of mud crabs moving through the system.

FRESHWATER FISHING

Moogerah Dam

SEQ Water has given notice that Moogerah Dam is now closed until further notice due to rising water levels and inflows.

Hinze Dam

Hinze continues to fish well, with bass holding in the backs of bays and along points throughout both the eastern and western arms. Spinnerbaits, chatterbaits and crankbaits are all producing fish, while 3-inch soft plastics on 1/4oz to 1/2oz jigheads are proving effective when worked off the deeper points.

Saratoga are becoming increasingly active. Anglers targeting the backs of bays, lily pads and weed beds with 90mm glidebaits, spinnerbaits and surface lures are finding fish on the move.

Maroon Dam

Maroon has been producing some excellent topwater bass fishing, particularly early in the morning and late in the afternoon. Small poppers, walk-the-dog stickbaits, surface insects and spinnerbaits are all accounting for fish around the edges.

Further up the creek arms, metal blades and spinnerbaits worked around the tree line and structure are producing plenty of bass. The recent inflows have the fish moving and actively feeding.

Wivenhoe Dam

Wivenhoe is fishing well, with bass and yellowbelly being caught throughout the dam. Billies Bay on the south-eastern side of the lake continues to produce fish, while the old riverbed, Platypus Cliffs and the Three Humps are all worth investigating.

For yellowbelly, Platypus Cliffs is producing fish on small blades, TN60-style lipless crankbaits and slowly rolled spinnerbaits. Anglers trolling lures that reach 5-7 metres are also finding success along the riverbed.
